# Why these rules (learning-science sources)

Condensed findings behind SKILL.md. Retrieval practice is strong, but only honest questions with feedback turn a quiz into a learning tool.

- **Testing itself teaches.** Practice quizzing beats re-studying with effect sizes around d ≥ 1.2, and works even without feedback; format (MC vs short-answer) matters little. → https://www.learningscientists.org/blog/2016/3/18-1
- **Quizzing only strengthens what it asks.** Quizzed facts transfer to re-worded versions of themselves, but "related" surrounding facts get no boost — cover key points explicitly. (Butler 2010) → https://www.learningscientists.org/blog/2016/6/2-1
- **Stems should measure understanding**, applying/comparing/causes, not verbatim keyword matching; no tricky or ambiguous items. → https://source.washu.edu/2018/09/for-better-multiple-choice-tests-avoid-tricky-questions-study-finds/ , https://pressbooks.pub/etsu/chapter/designing-meaningful-assessments/
- **Distractors = plausible-but-wrong**, ideally documented common misconceptions; joke options are dead weight; keep options parallel in length/grammar and avoid clueing. → https://sde.webspace.durham.ac.uk/writing-strong-test-questions/ , https://ijate.net/how-many-options-a-multiple-choice-question-should-have/
- **3–4 options are enough**; adding more options does not improve reliability and raises reading load for kids. → https://ijate.net/how-many-options-a-multiple-choice-question-should-have/
- **Right/wrong feedback alone does almost nothing**; elaborated feedback (why the key is right) boosts later learning and reduces the harm of exposure to distractor statements. → https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4073309/ , https://learninglab.psych.purdue.edu/downloads/2007/2007_Butler_Karpicke_Roediger_JEPA.pdf
- **Difficulty sweet spot ≈ 80% success** for a pupil who understood the text — effortful but not demoralising; wrong attempts can otherwise strengthen the misinformation. → https://www.learningscientists.org/blog/2016/5/5-1 , https://retrievalpractice.org/feedback
- **For classroom use:** low-stakes, frequent, spaced and interleaved quizzes improve results *and* attitudes while reducing test anxiety. (Roediger/Karpicke experiments summarised) → https://www.learningscientists.org/blog/2016/2/29-1 , https://www.learningscientists.org/blog/2018/7/19-1
